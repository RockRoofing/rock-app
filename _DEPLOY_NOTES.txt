FORECAST MARGIN - A NEW TAB
---------------------------
You were right that the data is already there. It needed no new inputs, only a
second read of the same records on different dates:

    revenue    the MONTH each sales line belongs to - the "Spread across the
               period's calendar months" grid on the forecast
    materials  DELIVERED (deliverDay), not paid
    labour     the END of each instalment's window, not its pay date

So the same forecast feeds both: a period paid in December for work valued in
September sits in September here and December on the cash flow, and both are right.


A REAL PROBLEM CAUGHT WHILE BUILDING IT
---------------------------------------
My first version put a whole period's revenue on its END DATE while costs spread
across months. That gave 90% margin one month and negative the next - true
arithmetic, meaningless commercially. Every sales line already carries its month,
so revenue now spreads exactly as the forecast says.

    Aug   rev 20,859   lab 17,000                margin  3,859   19%
    Sep   rev 20,859   lab  8,585                margin 12,274   59%
    Oct   rev 20,859   mat 42,441                margin -21,582 -103%
    Nov   rev 20,858                             margin 20,858  100%
    TOTAL rev 83,435   cost 68,026               margin 15,409   18%

MONTHLY IS STILL LUMPY and the page says so: materials are taken when delivered,
so one delivery lands wholly in one month. The TOTAL is the figure to judge. That
is accrual accounting behaving normally, not a fault - and better said plainly
than smoothed over.


IT CALLS OUT AN IMPLAUSIBLE MARGIN
----------------------------------
Above 35% it says so outright, with the arithmetic: at 20% the cost on your
revenue would be X against Y forecast, so roughly Z is unaccounted for. A margin
figure looks authoritative in a way a cash figure does not, so an unbelievable one
has to announce itself.

LEGACY FORECASTS ARE MORE ACCURATE HERE than on the cash flow - matDeliverDay is a
stored fact, whereas the payment date had to be rebuilt from an assumed term.

NOT INCLUDED: overheads, depreciation, accruals. This is GROSS margin. Overheads
from Budgets are the next step to operating profit.
