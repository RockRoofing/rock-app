YOUR SCENARIO, AS THE CODE COMPUTES IT
--------------------------------------
Valuation 25 Nov. Next period 26 Nov - 23 Dec.
Materials 20,000 delivered 26 Nov. Labour 32,000 over the period.

  materials in window          20,000  (exact, on delivery date)
  labour, working days 3/20     4,800  (26-30 Nov is 3 working days)
  accrued cost                 24,800
  already in November          20,000  (the materials - dated, so already there)
  cost ADDED to November        4,800  (labour only)
  revenue recognised           31,000  (24,800 / 0.80 at 20% margin)
  effect on net profit         26,200

Two rows at the foot of the table, under the WIP deduction, plus a "Net profit
after adjustments" line.

WHY ONLY THE LABOUR IS ADDED AS COST
-------------------------------------
Materials sit on their DELIVERY date, so a delivery on the 26th is already in
November's cost with no revenue against it. Labour sits at the END of its
instalment window, which for a period running to 23 December is December - so
the days worked in November are not in November at all.

The accrual is netted against whatever the model already placed. Adding the
accrued cost gross would count the materials twice; adding none of it would
leave the labour out. The drill-down shows accrued / already in November / cost
added per project, so the netting is visible rather than buried.

THE MARGIN IS COMPUTED TWICE, ON PURPOSE
-----------------------------------------
Those orphaned materials sitting in November with no revenue DEPRESS the blended
margin - the exact thing the accrual exists to fix. Left alone, the bigger the
year-end WIP the smaller the correction. So a first pass at zero margin
identifies the orphaned cost, the margin is worked out on the rest, and the
accrual then runs for real. In testing this was the difference between 0% and
20%.

Revenue is grossed up TO the margin, not marked up BY it: cost / (1 - m).
At 20% on 24,800 that is 31,000, not 29,760.

CONTROLS
--------
Panel above the table: include tick, and a box to type your own total which
overrides the calculation entirely. Per-project working shown underneath.

GUARDS
------
  - Only fires when November is still a FORECAST month. Once it is switched to
    Actual, Xero's own WIP journal is in the income figure and the existing
    include-at-year-end rule takes over. The two can never both apply.
  - Superseded periods excluded, same test as everywhere else.
  - If no period straddles 30 November the row says so rather than showing a
    bare zero - that would mean the forecasts have run out before the year end,
    which is a pipeline problem, not a fault here.
  - Balance sheet untouched by this.

WHAT TO CHECK
-------------
  - The per-project table: does the working-day split look right for your jobs?
  - "Already in Nov" should equal the materials for most projects. Where it
    equals the full accrued cost, that project's labour is already dated in
    November and no top-up is needed - correct, not a miss.
