1. A CORRECTION - pkg638 DROPPED COSTS IT SHOULD HAVE KEPT
----------------------------------------------------------
I applied one rule to sales and costs together. They are not the same:

  applied for                sales DROP   costs DROP   (real invoice + real bills)
  elapsed, not applied for   sales DROP   costs KEEP
  still future               sales keep   costs keep

If a period ends without being applied for you have not EARNED the money - so
dropping the sales is right, and conservative. But the WORK happened, so the
subcontractor invoice and the materials bill are still coming. Dropping those too
made the forecast look BETTER than reality, which is the one direction a cash flow
must never err in.

Costs now drop only when a real application replaces them. An elapsed period keeps
its costs until the actual bill arrives, at which point the month netting removes
them - nothing counted twice, nothing silently gone.


2. "CASH FLOW TO BE UPDATED"
----------------------------
Superseding happened SILENTLY. A period quietly left the plan and nobody was told
the remaining work had not been re-forecast.

The project's bar now goes DARK RED and a badge appears under the project name:

    CASH FLOW TO BE UPDATED

Two triggers, both meaning go and redo it:

  an application was raised AFTER the forecast was last saved
      - the position has moved and the forecast predates it
  a forecast period ENDED without being applied for
      - it has slipped, its sales are now dropped, the work needs re-planning

Hovering the badge or the bar says which, and names the application. A project
with everything still in the future stays clean.


WHY DARK RED RATHER THAN THE GREY "SUPERSEDED"
----------------------------------------------
Grey says "this period is done". Dark red says "the plan is out of date and it is
on you". Those are different messages and only one of them prompts action.
