YOU WERE RIGHT - IT WAS THE WRONG WAY AROUND
----------------------------------------------
The spread used the calendar months the period TOUCHES. Winnersh runs 24/08 to
11/09, which touches August and September, so it used the 09/08 and 09/09
applications.

BUT THE 09/08 APPLICATION WAS SUBMITTED A FORTNIGHT BEFORE THE WORK STARTED. Work
done from 24 August cannot be in it - it belongs to 09/09. And the two days from
the 9th to the 11th of September belong to 09/10, which was not in the list at
all. That is the missing tail you spotted.

    period 24/08/26 to 11/09/26, applications on the 9th
      BEFORE   App 09/08/26   App 09/09/26
      AFTER    App to 09/09/26   App to 10/09/26... i.e. 09/09 and 09/10

An application valued on date V covers everything UP TO V. So the ones that matter
are those with V on or after the period START, up to and including the first V
that reaches the period END.

Header now reads "App to 09/09/26", because it covers work up to that date - which
is the thing that was being misread.


YOU WILL NEED TO RE-ENTER THE PERCENTAGES
-----------------------------------------
The columns are different applications now, so a spread saved against the old
month keys no longer lines up and gets evened out. Your 60/40 will come back as
50/50.

The page says so in amber above the grid rather than letting it happen quietly -
silently replacing a deliberate 60/40 would be a poor way to treat a decision you
made.

Only affects forecasts on the Project application dates cycle with a calendar. End
of month + days is unchanged, because there the calendar month IS the period.


A CIRCULARITY CAUGHT IN BUILD
-----------------------------
appCalendarUsable reads periodMonths, and periodMonths now needs to know whether
the calendar is usable. Left as-is that is a value depending on itself. The usable
test now runs against the raw calendar months, which is the right basis anyway:
the question is whether the project HAS a calendar, not which applications one
particular period lands on.
