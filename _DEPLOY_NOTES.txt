WHY IT WAS 1,031
----------------
CIS was computed ONLY from bills already in Xero and individually ticked as CIS
labour. Nothing predicted it. So thirteen weeks carrying 224,686 of forecast
labour produced 1,031 of CIS.

Now it also comes off FORECAST LABOUR:

    net labour 25,585.57  ->  gross 31,981.96  ->  CIS  6,396.39
    net labour  4,000.00  ->  gross  5,000.00  ->  CIS  1,000.00

    your 13 weeks: 224,686 of labour  ->  CIS 56,171.50
    (the column was showing 1,031)

Still paid on the 22nd of the month AFTER the labour is paid, as before.


NOTHING IS COUNTED TWICE - TWO WAYS
-----------------------------------
1. Forecast labour is ALREADY NET of any real bill on that project that week
   (the billByProjectThisWk netting from pkg618). So a bill and its forecast
   cannot both generate CIS on the same money.

2. PAYE WAGES IN THE BUDGETS OVERHEADS ARE NOT TOUCHED. Employees are not
   subcontractors, and their PAYE is a different liability - the one you are
   paying down on the Balance Sheet tab at 6,000 a month. Deliberately separate.

Superseded forecast periods are skipped, same as everywhere else.


TWO SETTINGS
------------
CIS RATE % - defaults 20. Not every trade is on 20, and a gross-status
subcontractor is on nil. Set it to 0 and no CIS is predicted at all.

CIS ON FORECAST LABOUR - on by default, tick off to go back to ticked bills only.

The maths works from the NET figure: gross = net / (1 - r), so CIS = net * r/(1-r).
At 20% that is net x 0.25, which is what the existing bill calculation already did.


WORTH KNOWING
-------------
This will put a serious number into the forecast - roughly 56k over the thirteen
weeks on current figures - and it is money that genuinely leaves the bank. Expect
the closing balance to fall accordingly. If some of your subcontractors ARE gross
status, the rate is the lever, but there is currently no per-subcontractor
setting; say the word if that is worth building.
