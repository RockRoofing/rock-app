YOU ARE RIGHT, AND IT WAS A REAL FAULT
---------------------------------------
Both pages read salesSchedule.amount, which is revenueThisPeriod - NET of
retention. Correct for the cash flow, because retention is not received. Wrong for
the P&L, where retention is a DEBTOR: earned at the valuation date, simply not yet
paid.

    J248, one period
      certified gross            39,304.99
      salesSchedule carried      38,125.84   <- what the P&L was using
      understated by              1,179.15   = 3%, on every period

Now grossed up using each forecast's OWN retentionPct, not an assumed rate:

    stored as 3     -> 38,125.84 -> 39,304.99
    stored as 0.03  -> 38,125.84 -> 39,304.99
    stored as 0     -> unchanged

That handles the fraction-versus-percentage trap in both directions - retentionPct
is a fraction on some records and a percentage on others.


THE THREE STATEMENTS NOW TIE
----------------------------
  P&L revenue     39,304.99 gross, in the valuation month
  Balance sheet   debtor 38,125.84 + retention held 1,179.15 = 39,304.99
  Cash flow       38,125.84 on the payment date; the retention arrives later

Revenue equals what is collected plus what is held back, which is what makes the
three-way tie. Before this the P&L was 3% light against a debtors figure that DID
include retention - so "Out by" on the forecast balance sheet would have shown a
difference growing month on month, and it would have been genuinely hard to trace.

The cash flow is UNCHANGED and still uses the net figure - that is right there.

Both pages now say "gross of retention" on the revenue line, so the two figures
never look like they ought to agree.


AND ON YOUR ORIGINAL QUESTION
-----------------------------
The P&L and the cash flow will still not match, for three reasons that are all
correct: the P&L takes revenue in the month VALUED while the cash flow takes it in
the week PAID; the P&L runs Dec-Nov while the cash flow is 13 weeks; and the cash
flow nets real invoices off and drops superseded periods. Same money, different
questions.
