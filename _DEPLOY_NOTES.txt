FORECAST P&L - new tab, placed AFTER 12-Month as asked
-------------------------------------------------------
December to November, the same financial year as Budgets. Twelve months across,
each headed ACTUAL or forecast, with an FY total column:

    Revenue
    Cost of sale
       materials (forecast)
       labour (forecast)
    Gross profit
    Overheads
    Net profit
    Gross margin %
    Net margin %

ACTUAL months take income, cost of sales and overheads from Xero. FORECAST months
take revenue and cost of sale from the project forecasts on the ACCRUAL basis -
revenue in the month valued, materials when delivered, labour when done - and
overheads from the Budgets grid.

WHICH MONTHS ARE ACTUAL is decided by the Switch to Actual button on Budgets. That
switch is already the single decision about which months are closed, so this
follows it rather than having a rule of its own that could disagree.


COMPOSED, NOT REBUILT
---------------------
I started writing this as a server view and stopped. It would have duplicated the
whole overheads pipeline, and two tabs computing the same figure separately is
exactly the fault we have spent this session chasing.

It fetches budgets-overheads, margin and cashflow - the same endpoints Budgets,
Margin and Cash Flow already use - and composes them. So all four pages agree
structurally rather than by being kept in step by hand.


ONE LIMIT WORTH KNOWING
-----------------------
Xero gives ONE cost-of-sales total for a closed month, not the materials/labour
split. So those two sub-lines only carry figures on FORECAST months, and show "-"
on actual ones. Better than inventing a split.

Depreciation, accruals and corporation tax are not modelled, so net profit is
before those.


THE FORECAST BALANCE SHEET - NOT IN THIS PACKAGE
------------------------------------------------
You asked for it as the third leg of a 3-way. It is the right goal and I have not
started it, for a reason worth stating: a 3-way only works if the P&L and the cash
flow are both trustworthy, because the balance sheet is what ties them together -
it is where an error in either one shows up as a balance that does not balance.

Right now the forecasts imply a 58.5% gross margin because roughly 330k of cost is
missing from them. Build the balance sheet on that and it will not tie, and the
time goes into chasing the difference rather than into the model.

It also needs things nothing currently holds: an opening balance sheet by month,
debtor and creditor movements derived from the invoice and bill ledgers, and
retained earnings rolled forward from the P&L above.

My suggestion: use the Forecast P&L and Forecast Margin tabs to get the project
forecasts right first - the implausible-margin warning tells you when they are -
then the balance sheet is a genuinely useful piece rather than a reconciliation
exercise. Say the word and I will scope it properly at the start of a session
rather than the end of one.
