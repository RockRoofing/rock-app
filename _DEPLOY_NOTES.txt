YOU ARE RIGHT AND IT IS A CONCEPTUAL FIX
----------------------------------------
An application is labour + materials in a project period, valued at the valuation
date, and the remainder is the margin at that date. So cost belongs to the PROJECT
PERIOD, not the calendar month.

Costs were netted against real bills over a calendar MONTH. Where a valuation
period and a month did not line up - which is most of them - an August bill could
offset a September period.

Now:
  - cost is cut off at THAT PERIOD'S valuation date, not a month end
  - bills net against the period the cost was incurred in
  - the uncovered remainder CARRIES to the next application, exactly as sales do

    J240, valued 31/08, forecast cost 88,000, bills received 62,000
      26,000 carries into the next period

CASH DATES ARE UNCHANGED. A bill still pays on eom+X. This is about which period a
cost BELONGS to, not when it leaves the bank - which is precisely the gap WIP
exists to hold.


DO I NEED TO REDO MY CASH FLOWS? NO - BUT SOME NEED ONE CLICK
--------------------------------------------------------------
NOTHING NEEDS DELETING OR RE-ENTERING. This changes how the cash flow READS your
forecasts, not what is stored in them.

BUT: the valuation date is written when a forecast is SAVED. Any forecast saved
BEFORE you switched that project to "Project application dates" has no appDate on
its sales lines - so the boundary silently falls back to the PERIOD END, which can
be weeks later and puts the money in the wrong period.

THE PAGE NOW NAMES THEM. A red banner at the top of the Cash Flow lists every
forecast with no valuation date:

    4 forecasts have no valuation date - open each one and press Save.
    J240 - Market Drayton, J244 - Waungron, J246 - Albert Bartlett, ...

Open each, press Save, done. It picks up the project's own application calendar on
the way through - no re-entering, no deleting. When the banner disappears, every
forecast is being read on its own valuation dates.
