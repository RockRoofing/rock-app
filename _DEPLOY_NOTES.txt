YOU ARE RIGHT - A CONSTANT MADE IT PARALLEL TO CASH
------------------------------------------------------
I held availability still, so the available line was just the cash line plus a
fixed number. That is the opposite of how the facility behaves.

Bibby releases cash BEFORE the customer pays. So:

    raising an invoice   CREATES availability, weeks before the cash arrives
    collecting it        REPAYS the advance, so availability falls as cash rises

Now modelled week by week:

    + invoices RAISED this week    x advance rate
    - invoices COLLECTED this week x advance rate

    week  raised    collected   availability   cash    total available
             0       120,000              0    70,000        70,000
       250,000        40,000        147,000    30,000       177,000
             0        60,000        105,000    10,000       115,000
       180,000             0        231,000   -70,000       161,000
             0       200,000         91,000    50,000       141,000

The lines now diverge on a week you bill and close up on a week you collect -
which is the shape you were expecting to see.

RAISED IS TAKEN ON THE APPLICATION DATE, not the payment date. That is the whole
point: the availability appears when the invoice is raised, not when it is paid.

THE ADVANCE RATE IS YOUR OWN - totalAdvance over approvedLedger from the position
the IF page publishes. Not assumed at 70 or 80%.


ONE CAVEAT, WRITTEN INTO THE CODE
---------------------------------
This assumes every forecast invoice is fundable. Bibby will not fund a negotiated
job with no contract, may exclude a customer over its concentration limit, and
applies its own eligibility rules. So the dashed line is an UPPER BOUND on
availability, not a promise - which matters most on exactly the weeks you would be
relying on it.
