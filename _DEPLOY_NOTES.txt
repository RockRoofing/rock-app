THE ANSWER TO YOUR QUESTION: it did not, and now it does.

1. AVAILABILITY NO LONGER FALLS AS YOU COLLECT
-----------------------------------------------
Availability is totalAdvance MINUS drawn, so both sides move on a collection:

  the ledger shrinks    -> totalAdvance down by the advance rate   (was modelled)
  the advance is repaid -> drawn down by the same amount           (was NOT)

Only the first was in the model, so availability drained away through the
forecast. The two cancel, so it now stays broadly flat and moves only when
billing outpaces collection.

Once drawn reaches zero there is nothing left to repay, and a collection DOES
then reduce availability. The arithmetic handles that by itself.

Worked through at a 200,000 totalAdvance and 122,711 drawn:
  availability 77,289 -> collect 50,000 -> 77,289 (repaid 30,000)
                      -> collect 80,000 -> 77,289 (repaid 48,000, drawn 44,711)
                      -> collect 120,000 -> 50,000 (drawn clears mid-week)

2. BIBBY TAKE 60%, NOT THE WHOLE CHEQUE
----------------------------------------
The repayment took 100% of every collection until drawn was clear. Bibby
advanced 60% of the invoice; when the customer pays they keep that 60% and remit
the rest. Taking the lot starved the early weeks and cleared the facility far
faster than it really does. Now repaid at the advance rate.

3. THE REPAYMENT IS VISIBLE
----------------------------
It was netted off Invoices in, so the column disagreed with the ledger and
nothing anywhere said the facility had been repaid. It is now a line in
FINANCING & TAX - "Invoice finance repayment (Bibby)" - and the row adds up.

4. THE TOOLTIP FOLLOWS THE WEEK
--------------------------------
"less drawn" and "Card headroom" were both today's figures shown against a
future week. Both now move with the week.
