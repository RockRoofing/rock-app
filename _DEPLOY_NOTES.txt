1. YOU WERE RIGHT - IT NEEDED SPLITTING
---------------------------------------
"Project forecast" was a NET column: sales in, less labour out, less materials
out, rolled into one figure. So a week showing +174,821 could be 174,821 of sales
with NO costs at all, and nothing on screen would say so.

Now three columns: Project sales, Materials out, Labour out.

If Materials and Labour sit at "-" while Project sales runs high, the forecasts
have no cost schedules against them - and the cash flow is showing income with no
cost, which is exactly the shape of a closing balance that climbs to 668k.

That is the diagnostic. Deploy and look at those two columns first.


2. THE SUPERSEDE RULE HAS NEVER ONCE FIRED - MY BUG
---------------------------------------------------
pkg613 added a rule to drop any forecast whose period had already been applied
for. It read

    const apps = Array.isArray(p.applications) ? p.applications : []

but the dashboard cache does NOT have p.applications - applications live under
settings.applications there. So the array was always empty, latestAppEnd was
always '', and the rule never dropped a single forecast. The double count I told
you was fixed was still running.

dashboard.js now computes latestAppEnd - the period end of the latest application
- and the cash flow reads it directly. A new cache marker forces one rebuild, so
open the Commercial dashboard once after deploying.


WHAT TO LOOK AT, IN ORDER
-------------------------
1. Open the Commercial dashboard once (rebuilds the cache with latestAppEnd).
2. Back to Cash Flow. Project sales should drop where periods have been applied
   for - that money is now in Invoices in instead.
3. Check Materials out and Labour out. If they are empty against real sales, the
   project forecasts themselves have no costs scheduled, and that is a Commercial
   data question rather than a cash flow one - tell me what you see and we will
   work from there.
