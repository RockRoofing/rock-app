THE FASTEST WAY TO FIND IT IS TO STOP HUNTING AND RECONCILE
-------------------------------------------------------------
New panel above the customer table:

    Opening cash                                     30,000
    Invoices collected (debt you already had)       499,719
    Retention released                                    0
    VAT refunds                                      72,010
    New work billed and collected                   605,589
    Everything paid out                            -582,000
    Closing cash                                    625,318

    499,719 of the closing balance is collection of debt that existed on day one.
    434,338 of it is already overdue and collected in week 1. Strip it out and the
    quarter's trading movement is 95,599 - the figure to compare with your profit.

THAT IS THE ANSWER TO YOUR QUESTION. The 560k is not a bug. It is your ledger
being collected. A cash forecast SHOULD show that - but only if the money arrives,
and 434k of it is already late.

Strip it out and the quarter trades at roughly 96k against a 100k annual profit.
That is high but not absurd, and it points at the two things below rather than at
a broken calculation.


TWO THINGS WORTH CHECKING NEXT, IN ORDER
-----------------------------------------
1. IS 434k OF OVERDUE DEBT REALLY ARRIVING IN WEEK 1? That single assumption is
   most of the balance. Open the overdue row, look at the days-overdue column, and
   set expected dates on anything not actually coming. Every date you set moves
   money out of week 1 and the curve flattens.

2. COST IS STILL LIGHT. 605,589 of new work carries 419,889 of cost - 69%. Roofing
   runs 75-85%, so roughly 64,000 of cost is missing from the forecasts. Waungron,
   Christchurch and Russell Hill all show "none" for both materials and labour in
   the project table, which is where I would look first.

Neither is a code fault. Both make the forecast too optimistic, and together they
account for the gap you spotted.
