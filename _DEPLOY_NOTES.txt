pkg774 - Emails: won and lost projects in the matching
=======================================================

DEPLOY OVER pkg773 (contains pkg773). Thirteen files.

REPLACE:
  lib/crmEmailSync.js               (changed here)
  everything else as pkg773

FIRST, WHAT ALREADY WORKED
---------------------------
Manually assigning an email to a won or lost project was already possible - the
picker includes them, its placeholder says "won and lost too", and the server
does no status check. If you tried and it failed, tell me what you saw, because
that is a different fault from the one I found.

WHAT DID NOT WORK
-----------------
buildIndex skipped every non-open deal, so a won project could never be
SUGGESTED. Emails about a job you had just won - invoice chasing, variations,
site queries, which is most of the email a job ever generates - arrived in the
queue with no suggestion, and you had to search the name out by hand each time.

NOW
---
Closed projects are indexed and offered. They are still not allowed to file
themselves on a title match: a finished job must not silently capture mail on a
name it shares with a live one, which was the sound half of the original
reasoning. So it appears as "Looks like ..." with an Accept button, and waits
for a person.

A SECOND FAULT THE SAME LINE WAS CAUSING
-----------------------------------------
The `continue` sat ABOVE the contact and domain indexing, so those skipped
closed deals too - even though the comment beneath them says "Every deal now,
whatever its status" and explains why that matters. The comment described
behaviour the code prevented. Known contacts on a won job now file as intended.

AND A REGRESSION THAT WOULD HAVE INTRODUCED
--------------------------------------------
byEmail was first-writer-wins, which was safe while only open deals were in it.
With closed deals indexed, a finished job could take a contact ahead of the live
job the same person is working on - purely on array order.

An OPEN deal now takes a shared contact from a closed one, whichever comes
first. Between two of the same status, first still wins. Checked both orders.
