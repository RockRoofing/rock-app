YES - AND IT MATTERS MORE THAN IT LOOKS
---------------------------------------
Week 1 was carrying months of arrears and reading as an ordinary week. A separate
row makes both halves visible:

  Overdue - brought forward   in 45,477   out  8,000   net  37,477   closing 39,977
  w/c 24 Aug                  in 10,356   out 73,922   net -63,566   closing -23,589

Before, that was ONE row showing in 55,833 and out 81,922, with no way to see that
45,477 of the income was months late and might not arrive at all.

The row is amber, labelled "late - confirm dates below", and hovering explains it
is money past its date swept into today rather than money due this week.

Week 1 now shows only what is genuinely due in week 1.


THE CHART KEEPS 13 POINTS
-------------------------
The arrears row is not a week, so plotting it would give a fourteenth point that
reads as one and stretches the axis. It is folded into week 1 for the chart only -
same closing balance, combined in and out. The table keeps them apart, which is
where the distinction is useful.


CONSISTENT WITH THE TABLES BELOW
--------------------------------
Everything in that row is flagged "OVERDUE - confirm date" in the Bills to pay and
Invoices owed tables underneath, so the row tells you the size of the problem and
the tables tell you which items. Setting a date moves the item out of the arrears
row and into the week you actually expect it.

The 13-week totals still include the arrears - they are real money, just late.


REMOVED
-------
inWkOrOverdue, the predicate that quietly folded overdue items into week 1. Now
that arrears are split out explicitly, a single test that hid them would defeat
the point of the row.
