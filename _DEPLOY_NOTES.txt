THE LABOUR OVERRIDE
-------------------
labourThisPeriod was a bare derived value with nowhere to intervene:

  const labourThisPeriod = Math.max(0, labourToDate - prevLabourToDate)

"Labour this period" is now an editable box. Click Override, type a figure, and it
wins. Click Reset and it goes back to the calculation. The calculated figure is
kept on screen underneath while overridden, so you can always see what you moved
away from - and the box gets an amber border so an overridden forecast is
obvious at a glance.

Everything downstream follows the override: the month spread, the payment
schedule off the labour term, the labour-out cash stream, and the figure saved
on the forecast.

WHAT IT DOES NOT DO. Overriding labour does NOT touch the percentages, so revenue
is unaffected. That is deliberate - paying a gang early does not mean the customer
owes more. If you want revenue to move, move the percentages.

WHY IT WAS NEEDED. The calculation assumes labour is spent in exactly the same
shape as the work is valued. On a real job it is not: a gang goes on early, a
sub-contractor invoices in one lump, a week goes to weather. It is a decent
starting suggestion and a poor rule.

The override is stored per forecast (`labourOverride`: null means the saved figure
was calculated, a number means somebody typed it), so reopening a forecast shows
it still overridden rather than silently recalculating. "Revert to Application N"
clears it too, or the revert would be half a revert.


ALSO IN THIS PACKAGE (from pkg542/543/544)
------------------------------------------
- Forecasts seed from the latest real application, else contracted rates at 0%.
- Already-claimed value is deducted, so the whole job to date is no longer
  re-spread. Includes variations on both sides.
- A new forecast always deducts the latest certificate whatever period you pick;
  editing keeps the chain rule.
- Variations table: instructed in by default, uninstructed listed and badged,
  includable by tick. Value drives revenue, labour element drives labour,
  materials element drives materials - same treatment as the contracted scope.
- Materials on site already claimed on the last application are NOTED (money in),
  separate from the forecast's supplier lines (money out).
- "Revert to Application N" button - resets works, variations, MCD, retention and
  now the labour override.
- "Fill from line items" for materials, on the increment only.
- Backdrop click no longer closes the modal; Escape does.
- Banner saying where the percentages came from.


STILL OUTSTANDING, NOT IN HERE
------------------------------
variationValueToDate() in lib/applications.js tests `v.instructed === false`,
but instructed is the STRING 'yes'/'no' on this codebase - so an uninstructed
variation is counted IN FULL. Confirmed: one uninstructed £500 variation returns
£500. The cash flow cannot hit it (only ticked variations are passed, as
instructed: true) but it is live in the applications maths. Worth its own package.
