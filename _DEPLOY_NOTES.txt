HIGH INVOLVEMENT - SOLVED FROM THEIR OWN FIGURES
------------------------------------------------
Their guide, page 12: "your debtors will be funded up to a high involvement
percentage of the approved sales ledger" - each debtor capped at a share of the
APPROVED ledger, the excess deducted before the advance rate. Their illustration
uses 50%. Yours is 35%:

    approved debt      323,029.46
    x 35%              113,060.31   cap per debtor
    Wates              148,129.62
    over by             35,069.31   = their High Involvement, to the penny

At 50% it would be zero, at 25% it would be 67,372. Only 35% reproduces their
figure, and Wates is the debtor - 26.7% of your ledger on its own.

NOW CALCULATED, NOT TYPED. Everything it needs is already on the page, so it
recalculates itself and cannot go stale between statements.

The percentage is editable next to it, as you asked, with the resulting money
beside it. Underneath: "GBP 35,069.31 - Wates over by GBP 35,069.31. Cap
GBP 113,060.31 per debtor." So the figure is traceable to a name rather than
being a number with nowhere to go.

OVERRIDE. Leave the value box BLANK to use the calculation. Type a figure only
when Bibby's view differs - the box goes amber and says what the calculation
would have been, so an override never looks like the normal state.

'' is preserved as '' through the save. Number('') || 0 would have turned "use
the calculation" into a deliberate zero and silently killed the figure.

0% disables the deduction entirely rather than capping every debtor at nil.

TWO OTHER THINGS THE GUIDE CONFIRMS OR ADDS
-------------------------------------------
90 DAYS IS CONFIRMED. Page 6 defines the "agreed approval period (90 days)",
which independently confirms the age threshold I derived from their three age
disapprovals.

CASH CONVERSION RATE - NOT BUILT. Page 12: the CCR on every contract is 90%,
measured on whether the customer actually PAYS at least 90% of what you apply
for. Below that, NEW applications on that contract are not funded, though
existing debt keeps its funding. That is a per-contract switch driven by payment
history and is likely part of the 39,264.32 Contract Funding Disapproval. It
needs a decision on how it is measured before I code it - say the word.
