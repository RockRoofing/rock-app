YES, IT CAN BE DONE - AND ALMOST ALL OF IT IS ALREADY RECORDED
================================================================
Your W.L.A sheet IS the Planner grid, so I expected to be able to produce the names,
the dates and the jobs and no more. In fact the Planner already holds nearly the
whole workbook across five stores:

    ops:planning-allocations   project work, full or half day
    ops:water-ingress          call-out visits, with their own job names
    ops:overheads              Holidays, Sick, Paid Leave, Internal Time, Ops Support
    ops:overnight-allowance    nights away, by person and date
    ops:operatives-roster      names (Site App users take precedence)

Which covers every column: Employee Name, Date, Job No, Job Description, Hours,
Rate Description - one row per person, per day, per rate.


WHERE TO FIND IT
-----------------
Planner -> the Accounts report modal -> "Download timesheet CSV", beside the
existing Download report. It uses the SAME week commencing, the same number of
weeks and the same ticked installers as the PDF, so the two cannot end up
describing different people over different dates.

Dates come out as DD/MM/YYYY and the file carries a UTF-8 BOM, so Excel opens it
in a UK locale without turning dates into text or mangling an accented name.

TWO THINGS ARE DERIVED, NOT RECORDED - WORTH KNOWING BEFORE PAYROLL USES IT
=============================================================================
1. HOURS. The Planner records a full day or a half day, not a clock. A full day is
   8 hours and a half day 4. Somebody who actually worked 9.5 hours still reads 8,
   because the Planner has nowhere to put the 1.5. Your Hours & OA check sheet shows
   Michael Walker on 31 weekend hours, which is exactly the sort of figure this
   cannot produce on its own.

   If that matters, the Planner needs an hours field on the day cell. That is a real
   change, not a tweak, and worth deciding on separately.

2. WEEKEND RATE. Taken from the date being a Saturday or Sunday, which is how the
   workbook separates it. A weekday paid at a weekend rate cannot be told apart.


AND ONE HEURISTIC, FLAGGED
----------------------------
An overnight is often the night BEFORE the job - travelling up on a Sunday, or
staying over at the end of a holiday to start on site the next morning. On those
days there is no project allocation, so the allowance would land on "Internal time"
and the cost would never reach the job.

Your workbook does this by hand: 11 August is a Holiday, and the overnight that
night is booked to J246, where he was the following morning.

So the export uses the job that day, failing that the job within the next three
days, failing that the day before. It reproduces your 11 August row correctly.
Anything it still cannot place stays on "Internal time" rather than being guessed
onto a job, so it shows up to be looked at.

The Planner does not record which job an overnight belongs to. If that should be
explicit rather than inferred, it is a small addition to the overnight store.
