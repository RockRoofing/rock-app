1. WEEKLY REPORTS NOT SHOWING
-----------------------------
The metric is a PERCENTAGE of required, and "required" is derived from projects with an
ACTUAL day allocated on the Gantt that week.

No actual days that week -> required is 0 -> percentage is null -> the card shows nothing,
however many reports were written. And a report on a project with no actual day was not
counted at all, even when there was a requirement.

The page then made it worse by only looking at weeks where required > 0, so it skipped
those weeks entirely rather than showing them.

Now:
  - the API also returns completedAll: every report written that week, whatever the Gantt
    says
  - with a requirement, the card shows the percentage exactly as before
  - without one, it shows the COUNT, and says why there is no percentage:
    "Latest week: 4 completed. No projects marked on site on the Gantt, so there is
     nothing to measure against."

Worth knowing: if you want the percentage back, the Gantt needs days marked ACTUAL for the
projects on site. That is what defines "required".


2. EDITABLE DATE ON THE WEEKLY PROJECT REPORT
-----------------------------------------------
The Approval date was stamped with today and greyed out. Now a normal date field,
defaulting to today.

This matters more than it looks: a Friday walk round typed up on Monday was dated Monday,
and the Commercial Scorecard counts reports BY THIS DATE - so it landed in the wrong week
and the metric above under-reported.

I also had to stop the submit overwriting it. It forced today's date on completion, which
would have silently thrown away whatever you had just typed.


3. CUSTOMER FILTER ON STRIKE RATE
-----------------------------------
A "Customer" dropdown on the Strike Rate page, listing every main contractor with a
decided deal. Pick one and the whole page - rate, values, chart, detail - is that customer
only.

Deliberately NOT added to the shared filter bar. Strike rate against a named contractor is
a question you ask here and nowhere else; putting it in the global bar would have quietly
narrowed six other pages.
