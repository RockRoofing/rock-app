1. MARK-UP, NOT MARGIN - and it matters
-----------------------------------------
You were right, and the difference is real money:

    20% MARK-UP on £1,000 of cost   sell £1,200.00   profit £200
    20% MARGIN  on £1,000 of cost   sell £1,250.00   profit £250

£50 per £1,000. The builder was computing the second and calling it the first.

Now cost x (1 + mark-up). Checked: 25% mark-up on £2,173.20 of cost gives £543.30 of
profit, which is 25.0% OF COST.


2. NO DEFAULT MARK-UP
---------------------
The box starts empty and outlined red. The window will not close - not on the x, not on
Save, not on Escape - until a figure is entered, and the Save button is greyed until it is.

A mark-up that arrives pre-filled is a mark-up nobody checks. 20% sitting there on a job
priced at 30% is money given away without anyone deciding to.

New items no longer seed one either, which is what would have defeated the rule.


3. UNITS ARE A DROPDOWN
-----------------------
m2, m, nr, item. No typing.

Free text produced "m2", "M2", "sqm" and "sq m" on four lines of the same variation, which
is the sort of thing that only shows up when somebody totals a remeasure.

Starts blank, so a unit is chosen rather than accepted.


4. MATERIALS AND LABOUR ROWS HAVE A UNIT
------------------------------------------
Same dropdown on every line, so the workings read properly: 130 m2 of membrane, 6 nr of
tape, 3 nr of labour.
