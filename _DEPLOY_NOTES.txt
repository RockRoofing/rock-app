pkg413 - average secured project value for the estimators
=========================================================

Deploy after pkg412.

CHANGED FILES (replace)
-----------------------
  pages/scorecard-crm.js
  pages/api/targets.js

NEW FILES: none.


WHAT CHANGED
------------
On Roman's, Niall's and James's scorecards:

    REMOVED   Deals secured >=£200K
    ADDED     Average secured project value

Rolling 6 months to the month shown, with the previous 6 months and the direction of
travel underneath, exactly like Edita's:

    Average secured project value
    £200,000
    Previous 6 months: £50,000   ▲ £150,000 (300%)   from 2 won deals

THIS ONE IS PER PERSON. It uses only that estimator's own won deals - the same personDeals
set that drives their strike rate and GP margin. Edita's card is deliberately different:
hers is the whole business, because she is not an estimator and the figure on her card is
a company number, like the strike rate beside it.

So the two cards share a shape and a name but answer different questions, which is right:
"what is a job worth to Roman" and "what is a job worth to us".


THE RULES, SAME AS EDITA'S
--------------------------
Per WON DEAL, not per month. "What is a job worth" rather than "how much did they win" -
Total value of work secured already answers the second one, directly above it.

Won deals with NO VALUE are excluded. A won deal worth zero is a data gap, not a cheap
job, and leaving it in drags the average down for nothing.

Windows are exactly 6 months and do not overlap. Checked on the bench: Feb-Jul against
Aug-Jan, with a zero-value win and a lost deal correctly left out of both.


WHY THE OLD CARD WAS AWKWARD ANYWAY
-----------------------------------
"Deals secured >=£200K" was a monthly card with a quarterly target, rated on a rolling
three-month count through a special binary mode. It worked, but it was the only metric on
the page that needed its own rating rules. This one behaves like everything else.


TARGET
------
Default £150,000 per estimator - a placeholder. Set the real one with the pencil on each
card; it can differ per person, since the targets are stored per role rather than per
metric-per-person.
