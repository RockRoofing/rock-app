1. THE EMPTY-CACHE MESSAGE SHOULD NEVER HAVE BEEN THERE
--------------------------------------------------------
Every project on that page comes from dashboard:cache, which has a four-hour
life. When it had expired the page showed nothing and told you to go and open
the Commercial dashboard - a workaround dressed as an instruction.

The page now rebuilds the cache itself: if no projects come back, it calls
/api/dashboard (which is what rebuilds that key) and reloads. Invoices Owed has
always done exactly this.

The message only appears now if there are GENUINELY no projects with
applications after a rebuild, and says so.

2. NEW: INSURED LIMIT AGAINST WHAT IS ACTUALLY OWED
-----------------------------------------------------
You asked whether this was already built. Partly - the main table measures the
limit against FUNDABLE debt from applications. The new table measures it against
the real sales ledger: every outstanding invoice, whether fundable or not.

  Customer | Invoices | Owed | Insured limit | Headroom | Uncovered

Sorted with the largest uncovered exposure first, with an "add limit" button on
any customer that has none, writing to the same store as the CSV import.

That is a different and blunter question from funding: if this customer stopped
paying tomorrow, how much of it is covered? Over the limit is not automatically
wrong - it means the excess cannot be funded and is uninsured. Worth knowing
deliberately.

BE READY FOR AN UNCOMFORTABLE NUMBER. On the export you sent, the five largest
debtors alone - Wates 148,130, Epiphany 80,417, Baraa 77,207, Torsion Projects
73,904, Alheembouw 54,503 - carry 434,161 of exposure with no insured limit
recorded against any of them. Either those limits exist and have not been
imported, or that exposure is genuinely uninsured. The table will not tell you
which; it will tell you where to look.
