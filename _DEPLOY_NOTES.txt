YOUR THREE QUESTIONS
--------------------
SOURCE OF TRUTH - you reasoned to the right answer, and it is a hybrid. The
APPLICATION decides WHEN a period stops being forecast (the valuation boundary,
immune to invoice lag). The INVOICE decides HOW MUCH and WHEN the cash lands (Xero
as the record). The applied-for-not-yet-invoiced bridge covers the gap between
them. Using applications for cash timing would mean guessing payment dates;
using invoices for the boundary would leave the forecast hanging on until
paperwork appeared.

ROLLING - yes, that is what you meant and it is not what I had built. I dropped
the whole period. You are describing carrying the REMAINDER, which cannot double
count because the certified part is already a real invoice. You are right, and it
is the better model: work not certified this period slipped, it was not cancelled.

INVOICE GREATER THAN FORECAST - the same rule with the sign flipped. Certify more
than forecast and you are ahead, so the next period carries less.

Walked per project in valuation order, stopping at the first open period - so the
whole accumulated variance lands on the next period you are actually going to bill,
not spread across all of them.

IT RIDES ON THAT PERIOD'S FIRST SALES DATE, so it appears in the week that money
was already expected rather than being dumped into week 1.

VISIBLE IN THE DRILL-DOWN. A project showing more than its own forecast says
"incl GBP 55,000 carried forward"; one showing less says "less GBP 5,000 certified
ahead". A period that quietly grew by 55,000 with no explanation would be worse
than the problem it fixes.
